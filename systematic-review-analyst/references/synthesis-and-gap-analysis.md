# Evidence Synthesis and Research-Gap Analysis

## 1. Move beyond paper-by-paper summaries

A useful synthesis compares studies across shared dimensions. Possible organising lenses include:
- Theory and conceptual framing.
- Definitions and measurement of constructs.
- Population, geography, and cultural context.
- Platform, technology, or phenomenon.
- Research design and analytical method.
- Outcomes and behavioural stages.
- Direction, strength, and consistency of findings.

Use a matrix to identify patterns before writing prose.

## 2. Evidence synthesis matrix

| Study | Context/sample | Theory | Method | Constructs/outcomes | Main finding | Limitation/relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Author, year | Extracted evidence | As reported | As reported | Operationalised variables | Faithful summary | Source-grounded note |

Do not fill gaps in the source with assumptions. Distinguish author-reported limitations from limitations identified by the reviewer.

## 3. Types of research gaps

A gap may be:
- **Empirical:** a relationship or phenomenon has limited direct evidence.
- **Theoretical:** existing theories do not adequately explain an observed process.
- **Conceptual:** key constructs are conflated, inconsistently defined, or poorly operationalised.
- **Methodological:** dominant methods cannot answer an important question.
- **Contextual:** evidence is concentrated in particular populations, regions, platforms, or settings.
- **Temporal/technological:** the phenomenon has changed, making older evidence incomplete.
- **Practical:** evidence does not address a decision faced by practitioners or policy-makers.

A gap may span multiple types, but the argument should remain specific.

## 4. A defensible gap argument

Build the argument in four moves:

1. **Established knowledge:** What do the reviewed studies consistently show?
2. **Boundary of current evidence:** What populations, mechanisms, outcomes, or conditions remain weakly examined?
3. **Why the boundary matters:** What theoretical, empirical, or practical uncertainty follows?
4. **Contribution:** How does the proposed study address a defined part of that uncertainty?

Example structure:

"Prior studies have examined [established phenomenon] primarily through [dominant context/method/outcome]. However, the reviewed evidence provides limited insight into [specific under-examined mechanism or outcome], particularly in [defined context]. This matters because [theoretical or practical implication]. The present study therefore examines [bounded research question/contribution]."

Replace every bracket with evidence from the review. Do not present this template as a factual claim.

## 5. Avoid weak gap claims

Avoid:
- "No one has studied this" when the search may not be exhaustive.
- "There are few studies" without defining the evidence base and showing the basis.
- Treating an unstudied country as automatically a meaningful contribution.
- Calling a new combination of variables a theoretical contribution without explaining the mechanism.
- Claiming a method is novel merely because few papers used it.
- Inferring absence from a database search that used narrow or unstable terminology.

Prefer bounded language:
- "Within the studies retrieved and eligible under this review..."
- "The evidence identified is concentrated in..."
- "Few of the included studies directly measure..."
- "The reviewed literature offers limited comparative evidence on..."

## 6. Distinguish absence from inconsistency

A topic can be well studied but have contradictory findings. That is not an absence gap; it may be an evidence-consistency or boundary-condition problem. Ask whether disagreement is explained by:
- Different construct definitions.
- Different populations or contexts.
- Different measures.
- Different study designs.
- Different theoretical assumptions.
- Different stages of the behavioural process.

## 7. Link the gap to the research model

For every proposed construct or hypothesis, identify:
- Which reviewed studies motivate it.
- How the construct is defined.
- Whether the relationship has been tested directly.
- Whether evidence is consistent or mixed.
- What boundary condition or mechanism the new study examines.

The conceptual model should emerge from the synthesis, not be imposed on the literature after the fact.
