# Example: Virtual Idols and Tourism-Related Behaviour

## Purpose of this example

This case illustrates how to turn an emerging interdisciplinary topic into a transparent literature-review workflow. It is a methodological example, not a claim that the search described below is complete or independently reproducible.

## Known project parameters

The researcher previously described:
- Databases: Scopus and ScienceDirect.
- Intended publication period: 2015–2026.
- Language restriction: English.
- Screening arrangement: one reviewer.
- A ScienceDirect export containing 884 records.
- A working pool described as 159 candidate papers, of which 86 were prioritised.

The final search date was left to be confirmed.

## Critical interpretation

These figures must not be merged into a PRISMA flow without recovering their definitions and source records.

In particular:
- The 884 figure is a reported ScienceDirect export count, not necessarily the total records identified across all databases.
- The 159 candidate papers and 86 prioritised papers are working-library categories unless the researcher can demonstrate that they correspond to specific PRISMA stages.
- The Scopus count, duplicate count, records screened, exclusions, full-text retrieval, and final included-study count are not established by these figures.
- The final search date is unknown and must remain a placeholder until verified.
- One-reviewer screening should be disclosed; independent duplicate screening must not be implied.
- Do not claim full-text screening unless it actually occurred.

## Suggested next steps

1. Recover the original Scopus and ScienceDirect exports.
2. Confirm whether 884 represents exported records, a displayed result total, or a subset/batch.
3. Recover the actual query strings, field restrictions, filters, and search dates for both databases.
4. Establish whether the two exports overlap and document deduplication.
5. Define what "candidate" and "prioritised" mean in the researcher's existing workflow.
6. Reconstruct screening decisions from available logs; do not retroactively invent reasons.
7. Decide whether the existing process supports a systematic-review claim or should be described more modestly as a structured literature search and narrative synthesis.

## Possible synthesis dimensions for this topic

The following are candidate analytical dimensions to test against the retrieved papers, not conclusions about what the literature necessarily contains:

- Virtual idols, VTubers, virtual influencers, and AI-generated characters: whether studies treat these as equivalent or distinct phenomena.
- Fandom participation versus passive exposure.
- Digital immersion, parasocial interaction, and perceived authenticity.
- Destination image and tourism-related attitudes.
- Tourism planning actions versus stated travel intention and actual travel.
- East Asian platform and cultural contexts.
- Cross-sectional self-report designs versus behavioural or longitudinal evidence.

## Example of a bounded gap formulation

A defensible gap may emerge if the included evidence shows that research has concentrated on attitudes or stated intention while directly observed preparatory tourism behaviours remain under-examined. The review would need to demonstrate this pattern through its extraction matrix before making the claim.

A possible research question could then examine how engagement with virtual characters relates to concrete preparatory actions—such as saving destination content, searching for flights, or drafting itineraries—while distinguishing these actions from actual travel.

This is a candidate direction only. It must be revised to match the actual evidence found in the review.
