# Problem, gap, and question alignment

## Distinguish the components
- Phenomenon: what is happening in the world?
- Research problem: what important uncertainty or difficulty needs explanation?
- Research gap: what existing scholarship has not adequately explained or tested?
- Research question: what exactly will this study answer?
- Objective: what work will be done to answer it?
- Contribution: what will the answer add to theory, evidence, method, or practice?

## Gap validation
A defensible gap requires a synthesis of what is already known, points of disagreement, limitations of prior evidence, and why the missing knowledge matters. Do not equate a low paper count with an important gap.

## Alignment test
Every objective should produce evidence that answers part of the research question. Every research question should be answerable with the planned data. Narrow the scope if the population, constructs, or outcomes exceed feasible access.
