# Theory and conceptual model design

## Theory selection
Choose a theory for its explanatory function. State:
- what phenomenon it explains;
- what mechanism it proposes;
- what relationships it predicts;
- what boundary conditions matter;
- what it does not explain.

Compare alternatives where multiple theories plausibly fit. Avoid adding a theory label after the model has already been assembled.

## Model construction
Define constructs and their roles before drawing arrows. For each path, state the theoretical mechanism and evidence that would support or challenge it. Check whether the model implies temporal ordering that the design can actually establish.

## Model parsimony
Include a construct only if it has a clear theoretical role and measurable distinction. More variables do not automatically mean a stronger contribution. If measurement evidence challenges the model, revisit the construct definitions and relationships rather than forcing the original diagram to remain unchanged.
