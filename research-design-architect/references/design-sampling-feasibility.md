# Design, sampling, and feasibility

## Design selection
Choose the design that can answer the research question:
- cross-sectional survey: associations and group patterns at one time;
- longitudinal design: change and temporal ordering;
- experiment: effects under assigned conditions;
- qualitative design: meanings, processes, and contextual depth;
- mixed methods: integration of complementary evidence.

## Sampling plan
Specify target population, unit of analysis, eligibility criteria, recruitment channels, sampling method, expected exclusions, and sample-size rationale. Do not claim representativeness from convenience or panel samples without evidence.

## Feasibility
Assess access, platform capabilities, cost per valid response, recruitment speed, language, translation review, technical requirements, ethics conditions, data retention, and timeline. Build contingencies for low response rates or platform restrictions.

## Pilot purpose
State what the pilot is designed to test: comprehension, routing, timing, data quality, measurement behaviour, or operational feasibility. Define in advance what kinds of evidence may trigger revisions.
