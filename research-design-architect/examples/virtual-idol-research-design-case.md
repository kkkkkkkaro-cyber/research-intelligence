# Case: Virtual idols and tourism-related outcomes

## Research evolution
The project began with a broad interest in how virtual-idol fandom relates to tourism. It developed into a survey examining fandom participation, perceived immersion, and tourism intention across China and Japan.

The pilot (China, N=50) exposed item-level concerns, including the wording and interpretation of FP2 and TI2. The wording was revised before the main wave; the final analysis compared results with and without the pilot responses.

The original framework included destination-image constructs. Later measurement assessment raised discriminant-validity concerns, and the framework was revised to focus on fandom participation, perceived immersion, and tourism intention. The later model tested mediation and country-related moderation.

## Design lessons
1. Narrow a broad phenomenon into a measurable outcome and mechanism.
2. Distinguish tourism intention from actual travel behaviour.
3. Treat item wording as part of construct validity, not cosmetic editing.
4. Allow empirical measurement evidence to inform model revision.
5. Track changes between pilot and main survey waves.
6. Establish measurement comparability before interpreting cross-country structural differences.
7. Separate pre-specified hypotheses from exploratory moderators.
8. Keep claims proportionate to cross-sectional self-report evidence.

## Limits of this case
This is a methodological illustration of one research project's evolution. It does not establish universal design rules, and it should not be used as a substitute for reviewing the actual questionnaire, ethics protocol, or full analysis output.
