# Mediation, moderation, and cross-group comparability

## Mediation
Report the a path, b path, direct effect (c'), total effect where relevant, indirect effect, and bootstrap confidence interval. A significant indirect effect supports an indirect association under the model; cross-sectional data do not establish temporal or causal mediation.

## Moderation
Report the interaction coefficient, uncertainty, conditional effects, and probing method. Distinguish pre-specified hypotheses from exploratory moderators. Correct or qualify interpretation when many moderators are tested.

## Moderated mediation
Report the index of moderated mediation and its confidence interval. A confidence interval containing zero means the evidence does not establish a difference in indirect effects; it does not prove the effects are identical.

## Measurement invariance
Before comparing groups, assess the level of invariance required for the intended comparison (e.g. configural, metric, scalar), using appropriate fit-change and model-comparison evidence. Explain limitations and borderline results.

## Case illustration
The virtual-idol main study compared Japan and China. The revised framework tested fandom participation (FP) → perceived immersion (PI) → tourism intention (TI), with country-related moderation. Destination Image was removed following discriminant-validity concerns. The report assessed measurement invariance and repeated analyses without the Chinese pilot wave. Treat these as distinct steps: measurement comparability first, structural group interpretation second.
