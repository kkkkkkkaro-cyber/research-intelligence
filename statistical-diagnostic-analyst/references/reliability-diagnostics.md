# Reliability and item diagnostics

## What to inspect
- Construct-level internal consistency (alpha; consider omega or composite reliability when appropriate).
- Corrected item-total/item-rest correlation.
- Reliability if an item is deleted.
- Item distributions, variance, floor/ceiling effects, missingness, and inter-item relationships.
- The actual wording and theoretical domain represented by each item.

## Decision logic
- Low alpha is a signal to investigate, not an automatic instruction to delete items.
- A higher alpha-if-deleted value does not establish that deletion improves construct validity.
- A low item-rest correlation may reflect ambiguous wording, reverse coding, translation, a data-mapping error, multidimensionality, or a genuinely distinct facet.
- Before editing, compare the item with the approved questionnaire and original scale. Document whether the change affects comparability across languages or survey waves.
- If an item is revised after a pilot, identify the pilot wave and consider reporting analyses with and without that wave.

## Case illustration
In the virtual-idol pilot (N=50), FP had alpha .590 and FP2 had item-rest correlation .082; alpha-if-deleted was .651. The appropriate action is to inspect wording, platform examples, mapping, and construct coverage—not to delete mechanically. In the later main study, FP2 was clarified with country-specific platform examples. The reported corrected item-total correlation in China rose from .13 to .61. This is a case-specific outcome, not a universal expected effect.

TI2 was also flagged because “I plan to visit the destination after viewing the virtual idol content” expressed a stronger, content-triggered commitment than the other intention items. The later wording was changed to a more general future travel plan. Record this as a wording-alignment decision and account for the wave change in sensitivity analysis.

## Reporting
Report the coefficient, item diagnostic, item wording issue, action taken, and rationale. Avoid describing alpha as proof of unidimensionality or validity.
